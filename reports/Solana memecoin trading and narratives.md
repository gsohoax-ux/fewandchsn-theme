# Fees and graduation dumps decide memecoin bots

The Solana meme coin market is a lottery built on an automated, insider-dominated launch machine. Only about **0.2% to 1.7% of Pump.fun tokens ever graduate**, about **79% never trade after launch day**, and the median graduate is worth **3.8% of its graduation market cap 24 hours later**. The participants with a durable edge are developers and bundlers who control supply at block 0, not snipers, KOL followers or retail terminals. For a bot that scores coins and paper-trades a $30/day budget, three findings matter more than any narrative or momentum tweak. First, the bot's own cost model makes a roughly $6 ticket start about **15% under water**, so its "-25% stop" actually fires on an ~11% price dip, which is ordinary noise on a bonding curve. Second, the bot currently holds through graduation with no exit rule, straight into the period where the data says prices usually collapse. Third, the best-supported entry filters (social links, deployer history, entity-clustered concentration, non-insider buyer flow) are cheap to compute and only partly used. Narratives matter, but the evidence on how they rotate and which copycat wins is case-study folklore, not measured data. The honest posture is to fix the cost and exit mechanics now, add the well-evidenced filters, and let the bot's own logged trades settle the rest. One caveat governs everything below: the source notes were built almost entirely from search-result snippets because full pages (arXiv, Bitquery, Solidus, CoinGecko) could not be fetched, many key studies are unreviewed preprints or vendor analytics, and every number should be re-checked against its primary page before it becomes a hard-coded threshold.

## A 1.25% curve with 85 SOL at the end, and insiders at the start

A Pump.fun token starts on a constant-product bonding curve with roughly **30 SOL and 1.073B tokens of virtual reserves** against a 1B supply, and it "completes" when the curve's real tokens are sold out, which takes about **85 SOL** and has historically meant roughly $69k of market cap ([DeepWiki: pump bonding curve](https://deepwiki.com/pump-fun/pump-public-docs/3.1-pump-bonding-curve-mechanism); [Bybit wiki](https://www.bybit.com/wiki/article/pump-fun-solana-meme-coin-launchpad-guide)). The trigger is on-chain state, not a USD figure, so the dollar value of "graduation" moves with SOL; a bot should track progress in SOL or fraction of tokens sold, which this bot already does. Since **20 March 2025** graduates migrate instantly and for free to Pump.fun's own DEX, PumpSwap, rather than Raydium ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/business/2025-03-20-pump-fun-launches-dex-called-pumpswap-to-instantly-migrate-graduated-tokens-347360)). Fees have changed repeatedly. Third-party guides citing Pump.fun's fee page put curve trades at **1.25% per side** (0.95% protocol, 0.30% creator) plus a 0.015 SOL graduation fee, with PumpSwap creator fees tiered by market cap from 0.95% down to 0.05% ([Blofin](https://blofin.com/en/academy/education/pumpfun/pump-fun-fees-explained); [FinanceFeeds](https://financefeeds.com/pump-fun-introduces-dynamic-creator-fees-to-strengthen-memecoin-ecosystem/)). I could not verify the current schedule against the primary page.

The real cost of a trade is larger than the venue fee. Trading terminals add about **1% per side** (GMGN, Photon, Axiom before cashback), and priority fees plus Jito tips of about 0.004 SOL per round trip add another ~1.6% on a 0.25 SOL trade ([web3wagmi](https://blog.web3wagmi.com/best-memecoin-trading-tools); [toekn compare](https://www.toekn.com/compare/axiom-vs-gmgn-vs-photon)). Sandwich bots compound this: Bitquery counted **6.85M sandwich attacks** over eleven months, **96% of them on the Pump.fun curve or PumpSwap**, feeding on the 30-50% slippage settings memecoin traders use ([CryptoSlate on Bitquery](https://cryptoslate.com/solana-sandwich-bots-took-29-7-million-before-costs-bitquery-estimates/)). The fixed part of these costs dominates small tickets, which is the central problem for a $30/day bot.

The market's structure decides who wins. Pump.fun has regained near-monopoly share, with **78% of ~11.5M Solana launchpad tokens** from November 2025 to October 2026 and a peak of 90% in August 2026, after LetsBonk briefly passed it in July 2025 ([Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/investigations/solana-launchpad-year); [Unchained](https://unchainedcrypto.com/letsbonk-flips-pump-fun-in-daily-token-launches/)). Launch flow is automated: a non-dev wallet buys in block 0 on **38% of new coins**, and devs bundle their own block-0 buys on **21%** of them. Bundled developer operations extracted **at least $579M** against only about **$31.5M for independent snipers** ([Bitquery: snipers & bundles](https://bitquery.io/investigations/pump-fun-snipers-bundles)). That ~18x gap is the clearest statement of edge in this market: the party that controls supply at block 0 wins, and racing it for block-0 fills is a tip auction with thin returns. Retail flow runs through terminals, with Axiom alone at about **57% of Solana trading-bot volume** by June 2025 ([X: Adam_Tehc](https://x.com/Adam_Tehc/status/1930906164809826351)), so the dev-holding, bundle and top-10 numbers those terminals display are already seen by most of the crowd. A bot's realistic edge is not speed and not secret data. It lies in better filtering (entity clustering instead of surface metrics), disciplined exits, and avoiding the predictable post-graduation dump.

## Ninety-eight in a hundred tokens die, and graduates usually dump

Base rates vary by period and definition, but every source points the same way. Bitquery found about **1 in 58 Pump.fun tokens (≈1.7%)** had graduated over November 2025 to October 2026 ([Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/investigations/solana-launchpad-year)). Marino and co-authors measured **0.63%** across 655,770 launches in September-October 2025 ([arXiv 2602.14860](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14860v1)), and Kamat's survival analysis of 832,941 launches found a pooled **24-hour graduation rate of 0.198%** ([arXiv 2607.02823](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02823v3)). The 0.198% and 1.7% figures are not in conflict; one is a 24-hour window and the other is eventual graduation. Rates also swing with regime: 0.26% in June 2026 ([coinlaw](https://coinlaw.io/memecoin-statistics/)) and a reported jump to **6.7%** in the week after Pump.fun's July 2026 "BOOST" incentive change ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/defi/2026-07-29-pump-fun-token-graduation-rate-jumps-boost-changes-launch-incentives-409815)). A spike like that is a mechanical change, so any model trained before July 2026 needs re-checking after it.

Survival is just as bleak. Of 8,984,141 Pump.fun tokens, **79% recorded no trade after launch day** and only **0.2% had ten or more trades in week five** ([Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/investigations/solana-launchpad-year)). Solidus Labs classified about **98.6% of ~7M Pump.fun tokens** as rugs or pump-and-dumps, with only about 97,000 keeping $1,000 of liquidity ([Solidus Labs](https://www.soliduslabs.com/reports/solana-rug-pulls-pump-dumps-crypto-compliance)). Those are liquidity-outcome heuristics, not proven fraud; Chainalysis's stricter definition flags only about 4.5% of tokens, so the two figures measure different things ([crypto.news](https://crypto.news/chainalysis-over-4-5-of-all-tokens-launched-in-2024-have-pump-and-dump-traits/)). For a trading bot the practical reading is the same either way. Nearly every token ends near zero, so the question is not "is this a scam?" but "can the bot get in and out before it dies?"

Graduation is where the bot's current rules are most exposed. A non-peer-reviewed study of **49,118 graduates** found the median token's market cap 24 hours after graduation was **3.8% of its graduation market cap**; only **26.4% were at or above graduation market cap one hour later and 8.6% after 24 hours** ([MemeFees](https://memefees.com/research/memecoin-survival-2026)). Marino's paper notes that creators have structural incentives to sell before graduation and that concentrated sell waves cause abrupt late-curve drawdowns ([arXiv 2602.14860](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14860v1)). BOOST now routes about 20% of migration liquidity into automatic buys during the first ~5 minutes after graduation ([The Block](https://theblock.co/post/409815/pump-fun-token-graduation-rate-jumps-boost-changes-launch-incentives)), which plausibly creates a short window of support that sellers can exit into. Taken together, "graduation is a sell-the-news event" is the best-supported timing rule in the whole evidence base, even though the strongest single number comes from a vendor study I could only read in summary.

Trader outcomes match these odds. In the January 2025 Dune snapshot only **0.41% of 13.55M wallets had realized more than $10k** ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/300403/pump-fun-traders-millionaires)), and CoinGecko found the monthly share of profitable wallets rarely exceeded 50% and fell to **30.1% in June 2025** ([CoinGecko Research](https://www.coingecko.com/research/publications/pump-fun-traders-are-making-a-comeback)). Large "smart wallet" gains are often not copyable: of $752.5M in gains by 3,170 big winners, only **26% was genuine buy-then-sell trading profit**, while about half came from tokens transferred in ([CryptoSlate on Bitquery](https://cryptoslate.com/pump-funs-biggest-winners-may-be-impossible-to-copy-as-752-million-study-exposes-hidden-trading-gap/)). No credible, cost-inclusive backtest of any Pump.fun trading strategy was found anywhere. The bot's paper ledger, if logged well, would be better evidence than anything published.

## Narratives start in minutes, peak in days, and rotate faster each cycle

Every major Solana meta since 2024 followed a recognizable arc. Viral-animal coins ran from September to November 2024: MOODENG launched on Pump.fun and rose about 1,400% within weeks ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/362085/moodeng-memecoin-jumps-17-as-beloved-pygmy-hippo-celebrates-first-birthday)), and PNUT went from about $128M to about $1.3B after Binance announced a listing ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/solana-meme-coin-surge-binance/)). AI-agent coins followed; ai16z reached $1.5B by late December 2024 ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/markets/2024-12-29-blockchain-powered-ai-agent-ai16z-reaches-1-5-billion-market-cap-332546)) before the sector fell **75-90%** in weeks as TRUMP "sucked liquidity and attention" away ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/ai-tokens-down-90-from-highs)). The celebrity and political wave ended worst: MELANIA was down 90% within about 17 days ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-02-06/melania-memecoin-is-down-90-from-peak-as-novelty-value-wanes)), and LIBRA lost billions within hours after one cluster drained about **$87M in an hour** ([Bubblemaps](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/the-libra-playbook-how-one-cluster-drained-87-million-in-a-single-hour/)). Pump.fun livestream coins briefly became a meta after a September 2025 fee overhaul, but prices tended to dump after streams, with one coin falling 93% after earning its fees ([BeInCrypto](https://beincrypto.com/the-risks-and-opportunities-of-streaming-tokens/)). The 2026 market is fragmented and smaller, with total memecoin market cap around **$28-31B**, far below the late-2024 peak ([KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/meme-coins-still-matter-7-assets-and-launchpads-to-watch-in-october-2026)), and an X trending summary reported that no new memecoin had reached $1B in 2026 ([X trending](https://x.com/i/trending/2117286383765078458)).

Spread happens in a sequence the bot can partly detect. Coins appear within seconds to minutes of a viral post: a Grok name suggestion was tokenized on Pump.fun "minutes later" ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/309111?amp=1)), and copycats of a deleted Musk post in May 2026 appeared "almost instantly" ([Crypto Times](https://www.cryptotimes.io/2026/05/11/elon-musks-deleted-post-sparks-bmntp-meme-coin-frenzy-on-pump-fun/)). Organic winners then take hours to weeks to peak, and the biggest jumps came from external escalation: PNUT ran from the squirrel's death (30 October) through a Musk post (3 November) to a Binance listing (11 November) and a peak around 13-14 November 2024 ([Bybit Learn](https://www.bybit.com/en/learn/memes/what-is-peanut-the-squirrel-pnut)). Because the first token on any story now belongs to snipers and bundlers, the tradable decision is choosing among copycats in roughly the first 5-60 minutes, not being first.

How to pick the "real" coin on a crowded story is where the evidence thins out. No dataset compares "first deployed" versus "best ticker" versus "best community." The Block observed that Musk-name copycats on Solana had "much lower trading volumes but similar results," meaning they dumped too ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/340273/elon-musk-harry-bolz-memecoins)). The case studies suggest the winner is the coin that first concentrates attention and liquidity, often confirmed by a canonical signal such as the original subject interacting with it or a CEX listing. A ticker that matches the meme's literal word (PNUT, MOODENG, CHILLGUY) fits every 2024 winner but is untested folklore.

Social attention does have measured predictive value, but its source matters. Abnormal Twitter attention predicts same-day and next-day crypto returns, driven mainly by **user-generated posts rather than official accounts** ([CFR Cologne](https://www.cfr-cologne.de/download/workingpaper/cfr-25-02.pdf)). Influencer tweets deliver a short pop followed by significantly negative longer-horizon returns ([DL News](https://dlnews.com/articles/defi/crypto-influencers-deliver-only-brief-gains-for-investors/)), and Musk's meme-containing Dogecoin tweets were followed by **-9.43% abnormal returns** ([Kent](https://media.www.kent.ac.uk/se/29936/KheireddineAdeyemiCryptocurrancyimpactsofTweeting.pdf)). On-chain, one KOL's wallets bought before **61% of his calls, about 20 seconds early**, and typically sold within three minutes of posting ([Bubblemaps](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/bubblemaps-investigates-crypto-kol-moves-onchain/)). For a bot, a KOL call or celebrity post is therefore a signal to exit or to trade only the earliest minutes, not a reason to hold.

Rotation has accelerated. Animal and AI metas lasted weeks to about three months in 2024; post-LIBRA sources describe rotation in hours to days, though that claim comes from exchange research and an opinion blog rather than measurement ([Bitget News](https://www.bitget.com/news/detail/12560605111955); [CryptBull](https://www.cryptbull.net/2026/03/12/from-150b-to-31b-the-brutal-deleveraging-of-the-memecoin-attention-economy/)). The observable exhaustion signs are falling graduation rates and fees ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/343742/pump-fun-rapid-decline-graduating-tokens-memecoin-frenzy-fizzles-out)), the meta leader falling far from its high while copycat launches keep rising, and a competing attention sink (TRUMP draining AI agents; perps draining Pump.fun volume by about 80% in June 2026 ([CoinMarketCap](https://coinmarketcap.com/top-stories/6a3319f6e622116c9d28d72b/))). The bot's existing "hot themes from recent runners" tracker is the right shape. What it lacks is an exhaustion penalty and a leader-versus-copycat distinction.

## Early-flow signals work only after insiders are subtracted

The best-supported positive features are not the ones most traders watch. Kamat's survival analysis found launches advertising a Telegram channel graduate at **1.485% versus 0.166%** without one, about 9x, and launches listing all three social links graduate at about **17x** the rate of those with none ([arXiv 2607.02823](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02823v3); [Brave New Coin](https://bravenewcoin.com/insights/what-auditing-832941-pump-fun-launches-taught-me-about-memecoin-success)). The effect is correlational and scammers can add links, but it is cheap and large. Deployer history is the other strong filter: in a June-July 2026 cohort, tokens passing Scorplabs' dev-reputation filter graduated at **12.01% versus a 1.71% base**, roughly a 7x lift ([Scorplabs](https://trader.scorplabs.online/blog/pump-fun-graduation-rate-dev-signal-12-percent)), although that is a vendor result without peer review. Microstructure studies on the first ~100 blocks or first 30 minutes find that **capital efficiency, net buy pressure and larger individual buys** matter more than raw transaction counts ([Medium random-forest study](https://medium.com/@alleg88/predicting-pump-fun-token-graduation-with-random-forest-on-extremely-imbalanced-data-9ef8da928390); [GitHub capstone](https://github.com/tomdelcarlo-lgtm/pumpfun-graduation-prediction)). Marino's paper finds that tokens dominated by bot-like transactions have **lower** graduation probability beyond intermediate curve levels, and that participation by historically successful traders has only "modest and inconsistent" value ([arXiv 2602.14860](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14860)).

Counting early buyers is the trap. Kamat's study of coordinated sniper cohorts (1,012 persistent wallet rings across 166,098 launches) shows that the apparent lift from "lots of first-hour buyers" is largely arithmetic contamination: cohort wallets buy into the very outcome being measured, and the effect shrinks substantially once they are excluded. In **7.0% of cohort-touched launches there were zero non-cohort buyers in the first 30 minutes** ([arXiv 2607.02795](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02795)). The bot's "fresh" trigger (15 buyers) and its momentum weight on unique buyers both count insiders as demand. Wash trading inflates the same numbers: bots generate an estimated **60-80% of volume on some Pump.fun tokens** ([BeInCrypto](https://beincrypto.com/trading-bots-pump-fun-solana-meme-coins/)), and across chains **82.8% of high-return meme coins show artificial growth**, with wash trading typically run by about three actors per token ([USENIX Security 2026, Mongardini et al.](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity26/presentation/mongardini)). Volume-bot vendors openly sell aged wallets with randomized size and timing, built to defeat exactly the fresh-wallet and identical-size checks this bot uses ([openPR](https://www.openpr.com/news/4169480/this-powerful-volume-bot-is-helping-solana-memecoins-trend)).

Surface concentration metrics understate the danger. MemeTrans found that tracing bundles raises high-risk tokens' **true top-10 share by about 24% on average**, with coordinated accounts holding **36.5% of supply** on average ([arXiv 2602.13480](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13480v1)). The commonly quoted terminal thresholds, top-10 below 30%, dev below 5-10%, snipers below 10% with a red flag above 20%, and 10+ bundler wallets as a red flag, come from vendor guides, not out-of-sample tests ([Mobula almanac](https://docs.mobula.io/almanac/detecting-snipers-bundlers); [Meme Coin Navigator](https://memecoinnavigator.com/best-axiom-trade-filters-for-new-pairs/)). Bitquery notes its $579M bundle figure is a floor because **transfer-funded wallets evade simple funder checks** ([Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/investigations/pump-fun-snipers-bundles)), which argues for multi-hop funding-graph analysis over one-hop funder matching. DexScreener boosts and "Dex paid" show only that someone spent about $300, which ruggers can also do ([OpenLiquid](https://openliquid.io/blog/dexscreener-boost-feature-explained/)). Smart-money labels are retroactive and survivorship-biased ([Nansen methodology](https://nansen.ai/post/what-is-smart-money-in-crypto-a-detailed-look-into-our-methodology)), and academic work on copy trading finds DEX execution lag erodes copier returns and that KOL performance is non-stationary ([arXiv 2601.08641](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.08641)).

The bot already does more than most. It detects launch-block bundles by slot, clusters early wallets by shared funder, flags identical-size scripted buys, tracks dev selling and dev rug history, discounts dust buys, and penalizes round-trip wash volume. The gaps are that clusters stop at one funding hop, top-10 is computed on raw wallets rather than entities, unique-buyer counts include cohort and cluster wallets, social links earn only +5 each, and a dev's clean graduation history yields an "ok" flag but no score bonus.

## The bot's -25% stop is really an 11% stop

The bot's paper fills are deliberately pessimistic, and that is good, but it changes what its risk parameters mean. Every value check nets out the 1.25% venue fee on both sides, 3% slippage on both sides, and a 0.0015 SOL transaction fee on both sides. At an assumed SOL price of $150, a 20% slice of $30 is about 0.04 SOL. Working through the bot's own `valueOf` math, that position is worth about **0.0338 SOL the instant it opens, roughly -15% before the price moves**. The fixed 0.003 SOL of transaction fees alone is about 7.5% of the ticket. Consequently the **-25% stop fires after only about an 11% price decline**, the **+30% trailing activation needs roughly a +51% price move**, and losses shrink the next ticket (20% of a smaller equity), which makes fixed fees an even larger share. On a bonding curve where a single sell can move price several percent, an 11% stop is close to a coin flip on noise. The research notes' inference, built on these fee figures, was that stops should sit at -35% to -50% in price terms, sized so that a stop-out costs a small fixed share of capital ([Thrive](https://thrive.fi/blog/trading/memecoin-trading-strategy-risk-management-guide); [Odin autosell guide](https://odin.tools/blog/pump-fun-autosell-take-profit-stop-loss-2026)). These stop levels are practitioner folklore, but the arithmetic that makes the current stop too tight is not.

The model is also missing costs a real trader pays. It has no terminal fee, which is about 1% per side on the major terminals, and it uses 3% slippage regardless of whether the coin is being actively sandwiched. Adding a terminal fee would push the instant loss on a $6 ticket toward 17-18%. The structural fix is to trade fewer, larger tickets, or to accept that at $6 a trade the strategy must clear a hurdle of about 15-18% per round trip before it has any edge.

Exit timing has the next-largest gap. `paper.open` refuses to buy migrated coins, but a position bought on the curve is simply held through graduation and then valued at market cap × 0.98 with no special rule. Given the 26.4% one-hour and 8.6% 24-hour survival above graduation market cap, holding through migration is the worst-supported behavior in the current rules. Taking partial or full profit in the last stretch of the curve or within minutes after migration, inside the BOOST buy window, is consistent with everything in the evidence. The engine also stops tracking non-alerted migrated coins after ten minutes, and it is worth verifying that the trade feed keeps updating `mcSol` for held positions on PumpSwap; otherwise post-migration stops cannot fire until the 90-minute time stop.

Time stops are well justified by how quickly activity decays. With 79% of tokens dead after day one and survival above graduation market cap falling from 26% to 9% between hour one and hour 24, a 90-minute hold has a long tail of dead time. The practitioner inference is a progress-based stop: exit if the trade has not reached about 1.3-1.5x within 10-30 minutes on the curve, or has made no new high in about 60 minutes after migration. These numbers are untested defaults, not findings. Take-profit ladders such as "sell half at 2x, trail the rest, keep a 10-15% moonbag" are pure folklore with no backtest behind them ([AirdropAlert](https://airdropalert.com/blogs/take-profit-strategy-meme-coins/)). Sizing guidance converges on small risk per trade because Kelly sizing with noisy, fat-tailed win estimates is unreliable: 14 trades leave a 95% confidence interval on win rate of roughly 29-82% ([askrubyai](https://askrubyai.github.io/blog/posts/2026-02-17-kelly-criterion)). The daily $30 reset already caps total risk, so per-trade size mainly governs how much of each ticket fixed fees consume.

## Prioritized changes to the scoring, thresholds and paper rules

The table orders changes by expected impact divided by effort, and marks confidence in the evidence behind each one. **High** means the change follows from arithmetic or from large-sample measured data. **Medium** means it rests on preprints or vendor analytics with large samples that I could read only in summary. **Low** means it is practitioner folklore or my own inference and should be run as an A/B variant, not adopted outright. Parameter names refer to `src/config.js`.

Two smaller housekeeping points follow from the evidence without needing their own rows. The self-learning target (`LEARN_TARGET` 1.5x within 120 minutes) should be judged net of the ~15% cost hurdle, so a 1.5x peak is only about a +25% net trade. Graduation is also worth storing as a second outcome label, because it is the event the academic literature models and the one the exit rule above depends on.

## Conclusion

The evidence reframes what this bot is for. Scoring coins on narrative and momentum is the part every terminal and every competing bot already does, and the published models for it barely generalize across regimes. The places where the evidence is strongest are the unglamorous ones: the cost of each fill, the stop geometry those costs produce, and the predictable collapse after graduation. A bot that gets those right and filters out insider-inflated demand can at least avoid the losses that sink most wallets, which is a more realistic aim than finding the next PNUT in a 2026 market that has produced no new $1B coin.

The second implication is that the bot is positioned to produce the evidence this field lacks. No one has published a cost-inclusive, stage-by-stage, regime-tagged ledger of Pump.fun trades. If the bot logs entry stage, regime, costs, MFE/MAE and exit reason for a few hundred trades per variant, its own data will answer the open questions (best curve bucket, right time stop, whether narrative scoring adds anything after costs) far more reliably than the snippet-level secondary sources available here. Until then, every threshold in the table above should be treated as a starting hypothesis to be tested, not a tuned setting.
